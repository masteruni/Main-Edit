# Copy

Words on screen and in voiceover are where an AI film is easiest to spot. Read the line aloud as a person who has never heard of the product; if it could be on any other product's video, rewrite it.

## Rules

- **Concrete nouns over adjectives.** "Ships Thursday" beats "lightning fast". A number with a unit beats a superlative.
- **Specific to the subject.** Every line should be false if you swapped in a competitor's name.
- **One idea per line.** On screen: ≤ 7 words per line, ≤ 2 lines.
- **Lines connect.** Each line causes the next ("so", "which means", "until"), never a list of slogans.
- **Say it the way the medium says it.** A receipt says `QTY 1  REFUND  -$40.00`. A departure board says `DELAYED`. A tabloid says `SHOCK`. The medium's voice is part of the look.
- **End on the thing, not the brand.** The last line is the viewer's result. The brand can be the source of the medium (masthead, board operator, receipt header).

## Banned openers and shapes

- Introducing… / Meet… / Say hello to… / Welcome to…
- What if…? / Imagine… / Ever wondered…?
- Here's the thing. / Here's why. / Let's dive in. / Buckle up.
- Tricolon slogans: "Fast. Simple. Powerful." / "Build. Ship. Repeat."
- "Stop X. Start Y." / "X, reimagined." / "The future of X." / "X, but better."
- Ready to…? / Join thousands of… / Game-changer / Seamless / Effortless / Unlock / Elevate / Supercharge / Next-level
- Ending on a question.

## Voiceover

- Write for the ear: short sentences, contractions, one breath per line (≤ 14 words).
- Leave space: the picture should say at least a third of the film with no words over it.
- When the voice says a word, the picture should not also write that word (except captions).
